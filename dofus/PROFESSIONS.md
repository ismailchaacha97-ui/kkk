# DOFUS — THE PROFESSIONS GUIDE

### Which jobs actually earn, what they really pay, and which one you should pick

*Companion to `PLAYBOOK.md`. Read Part 1 (which job to pick) and Part 3 (the money mechanism).*

---

## 0. The one thing to understand first

**No profession is passive income.** Every one of them is either *time* (gathering) or *capital + knowledge* (crafting). There is no "level it once, earn forever."

And there's a second thing almost every guide gets wrong: **you don't make money crafting items. You make money turning items into runes.**

The chain is:

```
GATHER raw materials  →  CRAFT the item  →  CRUSH it into runes  →  SELL the runes
                          (thin margin)      (the margin)          (fast sale)
```

Crafting a level 200 sword and selling it is a low-margin, high-competition business. Crafting it for 200k of materials and crushing it into 500k of runes is the business. **Brisage (crushing) is the engine; crafting is just the input step.** Everyone who tells you "level Tailor to 200 and sell hats" is describing the part of the chain where the profit isn't.

The third layer, above that, is **Forgemagery (FM)** — using runes to improve items. That's where the biggest money is, because you're selling *skill*, not goods.

## 1. The honest tier list

Two separate rankings, because gathering and crafting are different jobs with different economics.

### Gathering professions — zero capital, start today

| Job | Verdict | Feeds | Reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Farmer** | ⭐⭐⭐ Best start | Baker | Wheat grows everywhere, always sells. Cheapest to level, most beginner-friendly. **And you need profession 50 for the Emerald Dofus questline — this is the cheapest way to get it.** |
| **Miner** | ⭐⭐⭐ Strong | Blacksmith, Jeweller | Consistently named among the most profitable in 2026 because high-level ores are in constant demand and few players bother. Downsides: travel time, competition at the good veins. |
| **Alchemist** | ⭐⭐⭐ Strong | Self-sufficient | Potions (healing, teleport, bath salts for FM) have permanent demand. Trade volume is enormous. |
| **Fisherman** | ⭐⭐ Decent | Cooking, Alchemy | Relaxing, steady, lower ceiling. Good fish sell well; the market is thinner than ore. |
| **Lumberjack** | ⭐⭐ Decent | Carpenter, Sculptor | Wood is always needed, but the classic woods are oversupplied. High-level woods are where the margin is. |
| **Hunter** | ⭐⭐ Situational | Butcher | Easy to level through normal play, but income is RNG-dependent on drops. Nice supplement, not a plan. |

**Cost to reach 200 as a gatherer: essentially time only.** That's the entire attraction.

### Crafting professions — capital required, real margins

| Job | Verdict | Why | Realistic income |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jeweller** | ⭐⭐⭐ Best overall craft | **Every player wears one amulet and two rings** — structurally double the demand of any other slot. And rings/amulets crush into the most-wanted runes. | Real reports: 300k–700k/day at level 100+ if you work the market |
| **Blacksmith** | ⭐⭐⭐ Excellent | Covers all slashing/blunt weapons since the merge. Excellent crushing potential; commission work is constant. | **460k–740k kamas margin per week** (real server report, March 2026) — note: *this is the honest number everyone else inflates* |
| **Alchemist (craft side)** | ⭐⭐⭐ Excellent | Potion demand is constant and the recipes are cheap to run. | High volume, low ticket price — good steady flow |
| **Tailor** | ⭐⭐ Good | Hats for everyone, but a crowded market. Best paired with Shoemaker for full sets. | 2–4M/day claimed by SEO sites — treat with suspicion |
| **Shoemaker** | ⭐⭐ Good | Boots, same story as Tailor. | As above |
| **Carpenter (Bowyer)** | ⭐⭐ Good | Strong demand from range classes (Cra, Sadida). | Decent |
| **Sculptor** | ⭐⭐ Good | Bows and staves — same buyers as Carpenter. | Decent |
| **Faconneur (Trophies)** | ❌ Not for you | Trophies/shields market is lucrative but the **leveling cost is brutal**: ~300k for 1–50, ~5M for 50–100, ~18M for 100–150, **~40M for 150–200**. | Great for veterans, a trap for beginners |

### The top of the pyramid

| | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Forgemagery (FM)** | 🏆 The actual ceiling | Margin of **30–100% per item**. A perfectly maged set sells for 2–5× the base price. And you can sell your *service* to other players — that's zero inventory risk. |

**But:** FM is built on top of a craft profession. You need Jeweller/Blacksmith/Tailor at a decent level first, because that's how you get the items to mage and the runes to mage with. And you should not touch it before roughly level 180 / profession 100+ unless you enjoy burning kamas.

## 2. Ignore these numbers

You'll find blogs claiming *"Alchemist: 5–10M kamas per day"* or *"Jeweller: 4–8M per day."*

Those figures are **invented for search traffic**. Here's the reality check against sources that actually report their results:

| Claim | Source type | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| "5–10M/day as Alchemist" | SEO blog | Fabricated. A very good day for an established player is a fraction of this. |
| "460k–740k/week as Blacksmith" | Real player report | Plausible and honest — that's the **margin**, not gross. |
| "340k–560k/day as Faconneur" | Real player report | Plausible for a veteran who knows the market. |
| "10M in a week from crafts + crushing" | Reddit consensus | Plausible at high profession levels with real capital and market knowledge. |

**Use these rough tiers instead:**

| Your stage | Realistic daily kamas from professions |
|---|---|
| Fresh character, gathering only | 50,000 – 200,000 |
| Profession 100+, one craft + crushing | 200,000 – 700,000 |
| Profession 200 + FM, doing commissions | 700,000 – 2,000,000+ |

Note what a "good" day at the top is worth: ~2M kamas ≈ **€1.80**. Professions are a *game-engine*, not a wage. They fund your gear and your progression — and they're the skill that later makes resale and services possible.

## 3. What to actually pick — for you

### The pair: Farmer + Baker

```
Farmer → grows wheat/flour  →  Baker → bread  →  sells to every single player
```

**Why this pair and not something flashier:**

1. **Zero capital.** You grow your own inputs. No HDV risk.
2. **Baker products are consumed constantly.** Bread is the most boring, most reliable seller in the game. Volume beats margin when you're starting.
3. **Farmer 50 is a quest requirement.** The Emerald Dofus questline needs a profession at level 50. Farmer is the cheapest profession to reach 50.
4. **It teaches you the HDV** — prices, timing, undercutting — with items so cheap that a mistake costs thousands, not millions.

Level it in **15–20 minute harvest sessions** in the Astrub fields. Wheat is everywhere. This is the least glamorous advice in this document and the most correct.

### Then add: Jeweller

Once you have 1–5M banked and level 100+:

- **Double demand** — two ring slots per player.
- **Crushing rings is the most liquid rune business in the game.**
- Buy ores from Miners (or level Miner yourself), craft, crush, sell runes.
- Add **Jewelmagus** when your Jeweller hits 65+ and you understand runes. That's the point where you stop selling items and start selling skill.

### The long game: Forgemagery

At level 180+, with a craft profession at 100+ and real capital:

- Build FM on cheap low-level items first. **Never practice on a 50M item.**
- Learn the "well" (puits) system before you spend money.
- Then sell **magus services** — "I'll exo PA your item for X." That's the highest-margin, lowest-risk income in the game, because you're paid for knowledge and your inventory risk is zero.

## 4. The rules that keep you from going broke

1. **Never level a craft profession by buying everything on the HDV.** That's how a 1–200 Jewelmagus costs you 5M+ and you never recover it. Level the gathering job first, supply yourself, buy only the gaps.
2. **Calculate before every craft.** `cost of resources + listing tax → value of runes received`. If the runes are worth less than the item, crush something else. Nothing else matters.
3. **Include the listing tax** in your math. A 5% gross margin becomes ~3% net — and 3% is a loss once prices move.
4. **Don't put more than 30% of your kamas into a single craft batch.** Ever.
5. **Crush, don't sell, low-level items.** Low-level gear does not move on the HDV. Crush it for runes and sell those.
6. **Check the rune market before you crush, not after.** PA/PM runes are where everyone piles in, so their coefficient is often bad — mid-value stats (crit, wisdom, resists) frequently have better margins because fewer players bother.
7. **Never craft a set you don't understand the demand for.** Off-meta trophies sit for weeks and immobilise your kamas. That's the #1 documented failure mode.
8. **Buy materials off-peak, sell finished goods on-peak.** Morning on weekdays to buy, evenings and weekends to sell. Prices for gathered materials dip on weekends when everyone farms.

## 5. The free-money trick most players miss

**The Craftsmen's Book.**

Once a crafting profession reaches **level 100**, get listed in the book. Other players come to you with their own materials and you craft for them — **free profession XP, zero material cost, zero inventory risk.**

This is the single best way to level a craft from 100 to 200, and it's how veteran crafters reach 200 without spending millions. The only cost is being online and trustworthy.

It's also a reputation engine: the Smithmage whose name everyone knows gets the commissions, the FM requests, and eventually the paying customers. **Being listed and reliable is worth more than being skilled and anonymous.**

## 6. Which gathering job feeds which craft

Pick your pair so you don't buy your own inputs:

| Gather | → Craft | Product |
|---|---|---|
| **Farmer** | **Baker** | Bread — the highest-volume consumable |
| **Miner** | **Blacksmith** / **Jeweller** | Weapons / rings + amulets |
| **Lumberjack** | **Carpenter** / **Sculptor** | Bows and staves |
| **Alchemist** | *(self-sufficient)* | Potions — sells directly |
| **Fisherman** | Cooking / Alchemy | Fish dishes, reagents |
| **Hunter** | **Butcher** | Meats for consumables |

**The best beginner pairs:** Farmer + Baker (safest), Miner + Miner-fed craft (most scalable), Alchemist alone (simplest single-job income).

## 7. Your plan

| Stage | Do this | Kamas |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1–40 | **Farmer** to 20–30 by harvesting Astrub fields. Sell everything raw. | 50–150k/day |
| Level 40–60 | **Farmer** to 50 (Emerald quest requirement). Add **Baker**, craft bread from your own flour. | 100–250k/day |
| Level 60–100 | Keep selling bread + surplus materials. Learn HDV timing. Start logging prices. | 150–400k/day |
| Level 100–150 | Add **Jeweller**. Start with cheap rings, crush a portion, sell the runes. Get listed in the **Craftsmen's Book**. | 300–700k/day |
| Level 150–200 | **Jeweller** to 100+, then **Jewelmagus**. Practice FM on cheap items only. | 500k–1.5M/day |
| 200+ | FM commissions + services. This is where the real money is. | 1M+/day, skill-dependent |

## The one paragraph

**Start with Farmer + Baker.** It's free, it's boring, and it satisfies the profession-50 requirement you'll hit at the Emerald Dofus. Then move to **Jeweller** — two ring slots per player means structural demand — and learn that the money is in **crushing items into runes**, not selling items. Get listed in the **Craftsmen's Book at level 100** so other players level you for free. Only touch **Forgemagery** at 180+ with real capital and cheap practice items. The players who get rich from professions aren't the ones who craft the most — they're the ones who calculate the rune margin before they spend a single kama.
